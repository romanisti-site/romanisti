# Reading Room RC4.1 — Canonical Architecture

**Status:** Constitutionally Locked  
**Role:** Canonical Reading Room master image  
**Source:** `design_artifacts/reading-room/Reading Room_RC4.1.psd`

## Architectural State

Reading Room RC4.1 establishes the completed institutional interior of Romanisti.

The locked composition contains:

- the full Reading Room architecture;
- natural light entering from the left-side windows and oculus;
- two Libri on the rear stewardship table;
- the certified Julius Caesar bust occupying the third position;
- one empty brass stand preserved as an invitation to future stewardship;
- five foreground editorial Libri;
- no carved or applied wall title.

## Locked Discoveries

### Architecture Before Branding

The wall inscription was removed. The Reading Room communicates identity through
architecture, objects, proportion, light, and restraint rather than explicit branding.

### Caesar as an Institutional Object

The Julius Caesar bust is neither a centerpiece nor decoration. It belongs to the
permanent architecture of the room and occupies the position formerly held by the
third rear Liber.

### Permanent Room, Living Interface

The five foreground Libri remain part of the canonical image. Their titles and dates
are architectural content within the room. The live repository interface may place
accessible, clickable HTML/CSS elements above the image without altering the master
architecture.

### Empty Stand

The empty brass stand remains unchanged. It signifies that future work must be earned
and that the institution remains open to stewardship rather than completion.

## Constitutional Boundary

Future interface work must occur around or above this architecture. The image itself
must not be altered without a documented architectural amendment.

An optional future presentation variant may remove the five foreground Libri while
preserving all other architecture. Such a derivative is not part of this patch and
must not replace RC4.1 as the canonical master.
