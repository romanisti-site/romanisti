Observation is the beginning of editorial work.



Not because everything observed is important, but because importance is difficult to recognize without first paying attention.



Rome requires this kind of attention. Its history rarely announces itself cleanly. The ancient and the ordinary occupy the same streets. What has endured is used, repaired, reconsidered and inherited. The past remains present without preventing the city from continuing to become something new.



AS Roma belongs to that continuing story.



Results, transfers, tactics and tables describe part of the club. They cannot explain all that it means. Roma also exists through memory, place, identity and the generations of people who have carried the club with them through the city and beyond it.



Romanisti exists to observe that relationship.



It sets out to report accurately, to provide context where context changes understanding, to recover what may have been forgotten, and to consider what events might mean beyond the moment in which they occur.



It does not seek to add volume for its own sake. There is already enough information moving quickly around football. Romanisti will be measured instead by whether its work leaves something useful behind: greater understanding, a recovered connection, a different perspective, or simply reason to look again.



That work requires independence, accuracy and restraint. It requires respect for the history being inherited and for the reader being asked to consider it. Above all, it requires remembering that neither Rome nor AS Roma belongs to any one generation.



And so Romanisti begins not with the assumption that it has something to say.



It begins by observing.
